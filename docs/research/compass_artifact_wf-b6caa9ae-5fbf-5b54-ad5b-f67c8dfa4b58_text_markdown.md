# AI in Software Quality Assurance & Quality Engineering: The 2025–2026 State of Play

## TL;DR
- **AI has become the default in QA, but scaled, trusted deployment remains rare** — nearly 90% of organizations are pursuing GenAI in quality engineering yet only 15% have reached enterprise scale (World Quality Report 2025-26), and developer trust in AI output has fallen even as usage climbs. The winning pattern everywhere is AI-generated drafts curated by experienced humans, not autonomy.
- **The QA role is shifting up the value chain, not disappearing** — from writing individual test cases to defining quality objectives, reviewing AI output, designing eval harnesses, and owning risk. QA is becoming the accountability layer for AI-generated code, and industry consensus is that AI augments rather than replaces quality engineers.
- **Agentic testing is the defining 2026 development, but it belongs at the top of a hybrid pyramid** — deterministic tests remain the stable CI foundation; agents (via MCP/CLI, e.g., Cypress Cloud MCP, Playwright MCP) add an exploratory/debug layer that is powerful but expensive (~$15–30/run at Slack) and non-deterministic. Phased adoption, human-in-the-loop review, and outcome metrics (defect escape rate, release confidence) distinguish success from the >40% of agentic projects Gartner expects to be cancelled by end of 2027.

## Key Findings

1. **Adoption is near-universal but shallow.** World Quality Report 2025-26 (Capgemini/Sogeti/OpenText, 2,000+ executives across 22 countries): ~90% pursuing GenAI in QE, only 15% at enterprise scale; 89% piloting/deploying (37% production, 52% pilot); non-adopters rose to 11% from 4% in 2024. McKinsey State of AI (Nov 5, 2025): 88% use AI in ≥1 function (up from 78%), but only ~7% fully scaled and ~6% are "high performers." ThinkSys QA Trends Report 2026: 77.7% AI-first QE adoption; 74.6% run 2+ automation frameworks.
2. **Trust is falling as usage rises.** Stack Overflow 2025 Developer Survey (49,009 responses, 166 countries, fielded May 29–June 23, 2025): 84% use/plan to use AI (up from 76%), but trust in accuracy fell to 29% (from 40% in prior years) and positive favorability dropped to 60% from 72% year-over-year. Verbatim: "More developers actively distrust the accuracy of AI tools (46%) than trust it (33%)," with only 3% "highly trusting" it. Experienced developers are the most skeptical (only ~2.6% "highly trust"; ~20% "highly distrust").
3. **Quality measurement is being redefined.** From pass rates/test counts toward defect escape rate, release confidence, cost-per-validated-release, and reallocated tester hours. WQR reports ~19% average productivity boost but one-third of organizations saw minimal gains.
4. **Self-healing and AI test generation deliver the clearest ROI** — vendor-reported maintenance reductions of 80–95% and payback of 3–10 months, though these are largely vendor figures and should be validated with a PoC.
5. **Agentic testing works but is costly and non-deterministic.** Slack's 200+ run experiment: agent runs cost ~$15–30 each; best config (Playwright MCP) 0% failure on simple flows and ~12% on complex flows; AI-generated tests hit ~48% failure on complex flows.
6. **Governance, regulation, and incidents are converging.** Gartner: >40% of agentic AI projects cancelled by end of 2027. Stanford AI Index 2026: 362 documented AI incidents in 2025 (up 55% from 233). EU AI Act enforcement milestone August 2, 2026.

## Details

### 1. IMPACT — How AI is changing QA roles, teams, and metrics

**Adoption statistics (the authoritative picture).** The World Quality Report 2025-26 ("Adapting to Emerging Worlds," 17th edition, published November 2025 by Capgemini, Sogeti and OpenText, surveying 2,000+ senior executives across 22 countries and 10 sectors) is the definitive QE-specific dataset. Its headline: nearly 90% of organizations are actively pursuing GenAI in QE, but only 15% have achieved enterprise-scale deployment. 89% are piloting or deploying GenAI-augmented workflows (37% in production, 52% in pilot). The rate of non-adopters rose to 11% (from 4% in 2024) — a recalibration after the initial rush, still far below 2023's 31%. GenAI ranked the single most important skill for quality engineers (63%), ahead of core QE skills (60%). Synthetic data use in testing rose from 14% (2024) to ~25% (2025). GenAI use is shifting from analyzing outputs (defect analysis, reporting) toward shaping inputs (test case design, requirements refinement). Average productivity boost ~19%, but roughly one-third of organizations saw minimal gains.

Cross-referencing broader surveys: McKinsey's "The State of AI in 2025" (Nov 5, 2025; 1,993 respondents, 105 countries) found 88% use AI in ≥1 function (up from 78%), 79% use GenAI, but only ~7% fully scaled and ~6% qualify as high performers (>5% EBIT impact). ThinkSys QA Trends Report 2026 (published Dec 22, 2025, drawing on 33 industry reports): 77.7% AI-first QE adoption, 74.6% running 2+ frameworks, and task-level augmentation dominates (50.6% use AI for test data, 46% for test case formulation, 35.7% for log analysis). The global software testing market is projected to grow from $55.8B (2024) to $112.5B (2034) at 7.2% CAGR.

**Role shift: execution → strategy/oversight.** The consistent narrative across WQR, ThinkSys, and practitioner communities is that AI absorbs the repetitive execution layer (boilerplate, test data, scaffolding, log triage) while human testers move up to risk analysis, test architecture, data strategy, quality objective definition, and governance. The work shifts from "writing every test case by hand" to "writing the goals agents operate against — success criteria, risk thresholds, coverage priorities — and then reviewing what they produce."

**QA as the accountability layer for AI-generated code.** With 84% of developers using AI and vibe-coding spreading, the volume of code needing validation is rising faster than human review capacity. Because developer trust in AI output is low (see Challenges), QA becomes the human accountability layer that decides whether AI-generated code and AI-generated tests are trustworthy enough to ship. The Jevons-paradox argument (more efficient coding → more code → more QA work, not less) suggests QA demand expands rather than contracts.

**Delivery metrics and how quality measurement is changing.** WQR frames the central 2026 story as reallocation of tester time from authoring to higher-value work. Traditional metrics (test count, pass rate) fail to capture AI's value (speed, coverage breadth, reduced maintenance toil), so the report pushes outcome metrics: cycle-time reduction, defect escape rate, cost-per-validated-release, and tester time reallocated. The reframing is from "how many tests pass" to "how much risk we reduced and how confident we are in the release." Practitioners describe language like "our defect escape rate dropped from 40% to 8% last quarter, cutting production incidents in half" as the new currency of QA value.

### 2. INTEGRATION — Workflows, toolchains, and agentic architecture

**Shift-left and shift-right.** Shift-left remains dominant per WQR, but shift-right (production monitoring, observability-driven testing) is gaining traction. QE is fully integrated into only ~20% of Agile teams, and ~50% of organizations lack centralized Test Data Management ownership — a structural weakness as AI increases test-data demands.

**MCP everywhere and CLI-first agents.** The Model Context Protocol (MCP) has become the connective tissue between AI assistants and testing infrastructure. Cypress shipped a Cloud MCP (generally available on all Cypress Cloud plans at no additional cost since May 20, 2026) that gives AI assistants a direct window into CI results, plus a Cloud CLI (cy-cloud) that exposes run status, failing tests, and the Test Replay timeline as JSON that "scripts and AI agents can parse." Microsoft's Playwright MCP and Playwright CLI are the backbone of Slack's agentic experiments. This CLI-first, MCP-everywhere pattern lets coding agents (Claude Code, Cursor, Copilot) monitor and analyze test results autonomously.

**The Cypress agentic stack (relevant to the user).** Cypress AI (built into Cypress from v15.4.0) includes: `cy.prompt()` (write tests in plain English), Studio AI (records browser actions and proposes assertions by watching DOM changes), self-healing selectors, Test Intent and Error Summaries, and UI Coverage test generation. Smart Orchestration (Parallelization, Load Balancing, Spec Prioritization, Auto Cancellation) is the "intelligence layer" that turns long sequential CI runs into optimized orchestrations; combined with infrastructure like Harness CI, a 30-minute suite can finish in a fraction of the time. Cypress also ships an open-source AI toolkit (skills/context for Claude, Cursor, Copilot). A recurring practitioner theme (Docker Captain/Cypress Ambassador Valdemar): AI can now write and heal tests, but pipelines must "validate no self-healed selectors" silently changed behavior — human review of healed tests must be enforced before merge ("The bot that ships the wrong build doesn't get fired. You do.").

**Agentic testing architecture — the layered/hybrid pyramid.** The strong 2026 consensus, validated by multiple engineering teams (Slack, Block, Scenario/LangWatch), is a hybrid pyramid: deterministic tests (unit → integration → deterministic E2E) form the stable, cheap, fast CI foundation; an agentic layer sits on top for exploration, debugging, and validating complex or UI-volatile behaviors. Key conceptual shift: "Tests enforce journeys. Agents verify goals." Because agents are non-deterministic, the top layers move from pass/fail assertions toward success-rate measurement and LLM-as-judge evals, and live LLM tests are typically run on-demand, not on every commit (too slow/expensive/flaky). Block's framing: pyramid layers now represent "how much uncertainty you're willing to tolerate," with CI validating deterministic layers and humans validating the rest. Some practitioners (QA.tech) note the pyramid is morphing toward a "diamond" as AI makes E2E cheaper and more reliable.

**Self-healing tests.** AI detects changed UI locators and updates scripts to preserve intent when the DOM changes. This is the most mature and widely deployed AI-QA capability, integrated into Cypress, Katalon, mabl, Testim, ACCELQ, Virtuoso, and others. Best practice: tune healing confidence thresholds (too loose masks real bugs; too strict wastes triage time) and keep an audit log of every heal.

**Tool/platform landscape (2025–26).** Traditional-framework-plus-AI: Cypress, Playwright, Selenium, Appium enhanced with self-healing and AI authoring; Katalon (named Visionary in the 2025 Gartner Magic Quadrant; self-healing, StudioAssist, RAG-powered context). AI-native/agentic platforms: mabl (agentic autonomous testing), Tricentis (consolidated Tosca, qTest, SeaLights under an "Agentic Quality Engineering" platform, claiming 95% regression efficiency for banking clients as of July 2026), TestMu AI/LambdaTest KaneAI and Kane CLI (natural-language authoring, 3,000+ browser/OS combos), Autify (Aximo agents), ACCELQ (business-logic-heavy), Virtuoso QA (NL + self-healing), plus newer entrants (testRigor, QA Wolf, Functionize, Shiplight, testers.ai, Applitools for visual). Tricentis Tosca remains the enterprise/SAP and core-banking strength; Katalon is broader in mid-market.

**Integration readiness best practices.** Keep AI-generated test code auditable and version-controlled (committed, reviewed, diffable). Avoid vendor lock-in (prefer tools that keep tests in open frameworks/code you own). Build infrastructure for token/cost attribution before scaling agents in CI. Enforce human review gates on self-healed selectors and AI-generated tests. Invest in Test Data Management ownership.

### 3. SUCCESS — ROI, metrics, and what distinguishes winners

**Documented ROI and payback.** Katalon's ROI framework for QA leaders reports 3–6 month payback when all four value categories are measured (labor cost reduction, quality improvement, velocity gains, strategic compounding), versus 6–12 months when only labor savings are counted. Self-healing vendors report the most dramatic figures: 25–50% reduction in automation upkeep costs (Quinnox), and Virtuoso QA claims 85% average maintenance-effort reduction (up to 95% for UI-heavy apps), pass-rate improvements from 70–75% to 96–98%, coverage expansion of 300–400% in year one, ROI often exceeding 300% in year one, and 8–10 month payback. A Virtuoso enterprise case reported use-case execution cost dropping from £4,687 to £751 (84% savings), £36,000 annual savings, and 385% ROI within 8 months. These are vendor-reported and should be treated as upper-bound marketing figures pending PoC validation. Note the wider enterprise reality check: McKinsey/independent studies find most organizations achieve satisfactory AI returns only within 2–4 years, and only ~6% see payoff in under a year — testing-specific self-healing/authoring is a comparatively fast-payback use case, but enterprise-wide AI programs are not.

**Metrics QA leaders should track.** AI revision rate (how often AI output needs human correction), maintenance ratio (maintenance effort vs. new coverage created), defect escape rate, failure classification accuracy (flaky vs. real), cycle-time reduction, cost-per-validated-release, and tester hours reallocated to high-value work. Baseline these before adopting AI — teams that hard-wire baselines reach payback ~33% faster (1.2 vs 1.6 years).

**What distinguishes success from failure.** (1) Strategic redeployment of saved hours — teams that reinvest freed time into risk analysis, exploratory testing, and coverage of critical workflows win; teams that just cut headcount stall. (2) Phased rollouts on narrow, high-value workflows (e.g., regression-suite maintenance) with before/after measurement, rather than attempting global ROI. (3) Human-in-the-loop review — the winning pattern is AI-generated drafts curated by experienced testers. (4) Clean data, modern integrated systems, narrow initial scope (6–8 month payback vs 12–18 for legacy/over-ambitious deployments).

### 4. CHALLENGES — Barriers, risks, and failure modes

**The hype-vs-reality adoption gap.** The chasm between strategic intent and successful implementation is real and documented. Practitioner analysis of r/QualityAssurance and survey data highlights a large gap between organizations calling AI a strategic priority and those that have actually adopted it for testing, with mandatory human review being a near-universal precondition for trust. The structural reason cited repeatedly: AI can generate test code but cannot generate test judgment — it doesn't understand business logic, context, or why a test should exist.

**Declining developer trust.** Stack Overflow 2025 (49,009 devs, fielded May 29–June 23, 2025): usage up to 84%, trust in accuracy down to 29% (from 40% in prior years); positive favorability fell from 72% to 60%; 46% actively distrust output, only 3% "highly trust" it; 66% frustrated by "almost right" solutions; 45% say debugging AI code takes longer than writing it. The #1 reason devs would still ask a human is "when I don't trust AI's answers" (75%). Experienced engineers (10+ yrs) are the most skeptical (only ~2.6% "highly trust"; ~20% "highly distrust").

**Hallucinated/incorrect tests and the self-validation problem.** AI can generate plausible tests that pass against broken code — a test that validates the wrong thing is worse than no test. A March 2026 Stripe benchmark (cited secondhand) found agents generate and execute well but break down when validating their own work. Consensus: QA should be among the last things fully automated; a human must check the tests.

**Flakiness and non-determinism.** Agentic tests take different paths to the same goal, trading reliability for adaptability. Slack's data (200+ runs, June 2026): AI-generated Playwright tests hit ~48% failure on complex "Search Discovery" flows (these tests typically progressed through 70–80% before breaking on a final interaction/assertion); Agent + Playwright CLI ran ~12–20% failure; the best config, Agent + Playwright MCP, achieved 0% failure on simple flows and ~12% on complex ones. Test flakiness remains a top QA pain point industry-wide.

**Token/cost budgeting for agentic testing.** Slack: ~$15–30 per agent run vs. pennies for scripted tests, ranging ~3 minutes (generated tests) to ~9–11 minutes (CLI). Token consumption for a single Search Discovery flow ranged ~3.5M (MCP + Sonnet 4.5) to ~7M (generated tests + Opus 4.6), with CLI ~6M and, counterintuitively, Haiku 4.5 burning ~5.7M — more than the larger models on MCP. Slack's key insight: "how the agent was executed mattered more than which model powered it," and MCP approaches used fewer tokens than CLI/Code-Gen because the dominant cost is context retransmission (stateless APIs re-send full history each turn). ReAct-style loops grow token consumption roughly O(n²) in steps. CI/CD amplifies this — a separate report documented an auto-triggered PR-review agent averaging ~400,000 input tokens per PR and $8,400 month-one. Mitigations: model-per-job-shape routing (cheap model for planning/scanning, expensive only for final generation), cost attribution/labeling, and running agentic suites on-demand not per-commit. Slack's own conclusion: agentic testing "may currently be better suited for targeted debugging or exploratory testing than for high-frequency CI execution."

**Governance and traceability of multi-step agent failures.** PwC AI Agent Survey (surveyed April 22–28, 2025, 308 US executives): 79% adopting agents, 66% report measurable value, but trust drops sharply for high-stakes tasks (financial transactions 20%, autonomous employee interactions 22%). Multi-step agent failures are hard to trace — which step failed, why, who approved. Gartner (June 25, 2025): >40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by end of 2027 due to "escalating costs, unclear business value or inadequate risk controls." Per Anushree Verma, Senior Director Analyst: "Most agentic AI projects right now are early stage experiments or proof of concepts that are mostly driven by hype and are often misapplied." Gartner also flags rampant "agent washing" — "the rebranding of existing products, such as AI assistants, robotic process automation (RPA) and chatbots, without substantial agentic capabilities" — estimating "only about 130 of the thousands of agentic AI vendors are real."

**AI incident growth and eroding transparency.** Stanford HAI's 2026 AI Index (released April 14, 2026), drawing on the AI Incident Database (AIID): "the AI Incident Database recording 362 in 2025, up from 233 in 2024" — a 55% jump; the Foundation Model Transparency Index average fell from 58 to 40/100; hallucination rates across 26 top models range 22–94%. Incidents are clustering inside aggressive adopters (organizations reporting 3–5 incidents rose from 30% to 50%), and self-assessed incident-response capability is declining (share rating their response "excellent" dropped from 28% to 18%).

**Testing AI systems themselves.** A distinct discipline: LLM evals, model drift detection, bias/fairness testing, and eval-harness design. Non-determinism means measuring success rates and trends, not pass/fail. ML/AI features must be treated as first-class quality concerns (accuracy, bias, drift).

**Regulatory pressure — EU AI Act.** The EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689) entered force August 1, 2024, phasing in: prohibited practices banned Feb 2, 2025; GPAI obligations Aug 2, 2025. August 2, 2026 is the next major enforcement milestone — the Commission's GPAI enforcement toolkit (information requests, model access, fines) switches on and Article 50 transparency obligations (chatbot disclosure, AI-content marking, deepfake labeling) become enforceable. Penalties: up to €35M or 7% of global turnover (prohibited practices); €15M or 3% (high-risk/GPAI non-compliance); €7.5M or 1% (incorrect info). Important nuance: June 16, 2026 "Digital Omnibus" amendments pushed most high-risk Annex III obligations to December 2, 2027 and Annex I to August 2, 2028 — so the heaviest high-risk obligations shifted, but Aug 2, 2026 remains a hard date for transparency and GPAI enforcement. For regulated-sector QA, this intensifies traceability and audit demands.

**Other barriers.** WQR 2025 top challenges: data privacy risks (67%), integration complexity (64%), hallucination/reliability (60%) — a shift from 2024's more strategic obstacles (validation strategy 50%, AI skills 42%, undefined QE org 41%). 50% of organizations lack AI/ML expertise (unchanged from 2024). Skills gaps (prompt engineering, agent orchestration, LLM behavior), cultural resistance/job-displacement fears, and tool fragmentation all persist.

### 5. INDUSTRY EXPERTISE — Skills and how roles are evolving

**Consensus: augmentation, not replacement.** Across WQR, McKinsey, vendor and practitioner sources, the dominant view is that AI augments rather than replaces QA engineers. Human judgment remains irreplaceable for exploratory testing (finding novel edge cases), risk judgment (which failing test blocks a release — a business call, not a probability score), business-logic and context understanding, and quality advocacy.

**QA → SDET/quality engineer with AI-oversight skills.** The role is evolving toward SDET/quality engineer who can review AI-generated code, design eval harnesses, set risk-based test strategy, and govern AI-generated output. Domain expertise is repeatedly cited as the key differentiator: AI can generate test code but not test judgment.

**New skill requirements.** Reviewing AI-generated code/tests critically; eval-harness and LLM-as-judge design; risk-based test strategy; prompt engineering and writing testable quality objectives; agent orchestration and understanding LLM behavior/non-determinism; and communication/quality advocacy (soft skills ranked 5th most critical at 51% in WQR).

**Certifications and upskilling.** ISTQB has moved quickly: the Certified Tester AI Testing (CT-AI) certification (with a v2.0; v1.0 English retiring April 21, 2027) covers testing AI-based systems (non-determinism, bias, explainability, ethics). ISTQB also approved a new specialist certification, Certified Tester – Testing with Generative AI (CT-GenAI), by General Assembly on July 25, 2025. 58% of enterprises are actively upskilling QA teams in AI tools, cloud, and security testing (WQR).

**Sector-specific perspectives (regulated industries).** In finance, healthcare, telecom, and aerospace/defense, quality ties directly to compliance and business continuity, so "faster testing still has to be defensible." Regulated software demands traceability, determinism, and independent verification — three properties that sit in direct tension with generative AI's probabilistic nature. McKinsey notes AI "does not remove the need for compliance but intensifies it." Requirements auditors probe: prove critical workflows were tested pre-release; prove who changed what/when/approved; prove test evidence is authentic and reproducible; prove sensitive data wasn't exposed (SOX, HIPAA, PCI DSS, GDPR, FDA software guidance). Leading regulated organizations embed automated compliance checkpoints, immutable audit logs, cryptographic signatures, and metadata tagging into CI/CD. The EU AI Act's Annex III captures most regulated-industry AI (credit scoring, insurance underwriting, healthcare diagnostics, employment decisions). Finance emphasizes PCI DSS/SOX and core-banking modernization (Tricentis Tosca strength); healthcare faces intense FDA scrutiny — ECRI ranked AI the #1 health-technology hazard for 2025, with CEO Marc Schabacker warning that "the promise of artificial intelligence's capabilities must not distract us from its risks or its ability to harm patients and providers" and that "AI is only as good as the data it is given and the guardrails that govern its use"; aerospace/defense faces shrinking teams and compressed timelines against rigorous standards.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Foundations (0–3 months).**
- Baseline outcome metrics NOW, before adopting AI: defect escape rate, maintenance ratio, cycle time, cost-per-validated-release, tester hours by activity. This alone accelerates payback ~33%.
- Reframe QA reporting from pass rates/test counts to risk reduction and release confidence.
- Fix Test Data Management ownership (50% of orgs lack it) and ensure all test code — including AI-generated — is version-controlled, reviewed, and diffable.
- Pick ONE narrow, high-value workflow (e.g., regression-suite maintenance or test-data generation) for the first AI pilot; measure before/after.

**Stage 2 — Integrate AI into the existing stack (3–9 months).**
- Deploy self-healing and AI-assisted authoring within your current framework (Cypress `cy.prompt()`/Studio AI, or equivalents) — the highest-ROI, most-mature capabilities. Enforce a CI gate that flags/blocks silently self-healed selectors for human review.
- Adopt the hybrid pyramid: keep deterministic tests as the CI foundation; do NOT run agentic/LLM tests on every commit.
- Stand up token/cost attribution before any agent runs in CI. Budget in both dollars and tokens; use model-per-job-shape routing.
- Keep human-in-the-loop review mandatory for AI-generated tests — never let AI validate AI unsupervised.

**Stage 3 — Introduce the agentic layer selectively (9–18 months).**
- Use agentic testing (Cypress Cloud MCP/cy-cloud, Playwright MCP, or a platform like mabl/KaneAI/Tricentis) for exploration, flaky-workflow debugging, and reproducing production bugs — on-demand, not high-frequency CI. Expect ~$15–30/run and non-determinism; measure success rates, not pass/fail.
- Build eval harnesses for any AI features in your own product (accuracy, bias, drift, LLM-as-judge).
- For regulated products, require vendor SOC 2/ISO attestation, immutable audit logs, and reproducible test evidence; keep a defensible human-approval trail ahead of the EU AI Act's August 2, 2026 milestone.

**Skills to build (continuous).**
- Upskill the team on reviewing AI output, eval design, risk-based strategy, prompt engineering, and agent orchestration. Pursue ISTQB CT-AI and CT-GenAI.
- Redeploy hours saved by AI into risk analysis, exploratory testing, and coverage of your ten most business-critical workflows — this reinvestment is what separates winners from stalled adopters.

**Benchmarks that should change the plan.**
- If AI revision rate stays high (AI output routinely needs rewriting) → keep AI confined to boilerplate/low-stakes; do not expand.
- If agentic CI token costs exceed the labor they replace → pull agents back to on-demand exploratory use.
- If defect escape rate rises after AI adoption → AI is generating false confidence; reinstate stricter human review.
- If self-healing masks real regressions → tighten healing confidence thresholds and audit heal logs.

## Caveats
- **Vendor vs. independent data.** The most dramatic ROI and self-healing figures (85–95% maintenance reduction, 300–400% ROI) come from vendor marketing (Virtuoso, Katalon, Tricentis) and should be validated with your own PoC. Authoritative independent anchors are the World Quality Report, McKinsey, Stack Overflow, Stanford AI Index, Gartner, and PwC.
- **Optimistic vs. skeptical narratives diverge sharply.** Vendors emphasize autonomy and speed; practitioners (Stack Overflow, r/QualityAssurance, experienced SDETs) emphasize trust erosion, hallucinated tests, and the necessity of human review. Both are represented here deliberately.
- **The Gartner >40% cancellation figure is a June 25, 2025 prediction, not a measured outcome** — it is frequently re-quoted undated in 2026 coverage (e.g., a July 7, 2026 Forbes analysis) as if new.
- **Slack's agentic figures are approximate, single-company, and explicitly "not optimized"** for token usage; costs may fall with future models/tooling.
- **EU AI Act dates are in flux** — the June 16, 2026 Digital Omnibus amendments moved most high-risk obligations to 2027–2028; confirm current status against official EU sources before compliance planning.
- **Some secondary statistics** (e.g., the Stripe self-validation benchmark, certain adoption percentages, and market-size projections) were found via secondhand coverage rather than primary sources and are flagged as such in-text.
